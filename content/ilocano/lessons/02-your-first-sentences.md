# Your First Sentences

> **The one-sentence version:** With about a dozen phrases (**Kumustaka?**, **Naimbag met**, **Agyamanak**, **Wen** and **Saan**, and **Ania ti naganmo?**) you can greet anyone, introduce yourself and say thank you, which covers most of a first conversation.

## Why it matters

The first thirty seconds decide how a conversation goes. Ilocano speakers, especially older ones, light up when someone makes the effort, and these phrases are the ones you'll use every single day. Learn them as whole chunks first; lessons 3 and 4 take them apart.

## Greetings

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kumustaka?** | How are you? (to one person) |
| **Kumustakayo?** | How are you? (to several people, or politely to one) |
| **Naimbag nga aldaw.** | Good day. |
| **Naimbag a bigat.** | Good morning. |
| **Naimbag a malem.** | Good afternoon. |
| **Naimbag a rabii.** | Good evening / good night. |

**Kumusta** comes from Spanish *¿cómo está?*, and the pronoun ending **-ka** ("you") sticks straight onto it.

> 💡 You'll also hear the greetings aimed at the listener: **Naimbag a bigatmo** ("your good morning", to one person) and **Naimbag a bigatyo** (to several, or politely). Both are correct; the bare version is the safe all-purpose one.

## Answering "How are you?"

> **A:** Kumustaka?
> **B:** **Naimbag met, agyamanak. Sika?** — "I'm fine too, thank you. And you?"

- **naimbag** = good, fine.
- **met** = "too, also". It also softens a reply, so **Naimbag met** feels warmer than a bare *naimbag*.
- **sika** = you, the stand-alone form you use when "you" is the whole answer.

To an elder, switch to the plural **kayo** and add **apo** (lesson 12): **Naimbag met, apo. Dakayo?**

## Yes, no, and thank you

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wen.** | Yes. |
| **Saan.** / **Haan.** | No. (*Haan* is the everyday spoken form) |
| **Agyamanak.** | Thank you. (also spelled *agyamannak*) |
| **Agyamanak unay.** | Thank you very much. |
| **Dios ti agngina.** | "God will repay you": a deep, heartfelt thank-you |
| **Awan ti anianna.** | It's nothing / you're welcome |
| **Pakawanennak.** | I'm sorry / forgive me. |

> ⚠️ If you know Tagalog: **saan** in Ilocano means **no**, not "where". It's the single most famous trap between the two languages.

## Names and where you're from

> **Ania ti naganmo?** — "What's your name?" (*ania* = what, *nagan* = name, *-mo* = your)
> **Ti naganko ket Miguel.** — "My name is Miguel."
> **Siak ni Miguel.** — "I'm Miguel."

**ni** is the marker for a person's name (lesson 4), and **siak** is the stand-alone "I". **ket** links a topic to what's said about it, like a spoken "is" here.

> **Taga-anoka?** — "Where are you from?"
> **Taga-Vigan ak.** / **Taga-Honolulu ak.** — "I'm from Vigan / Honolulu."

**Taga-** + a place means "from". Learners often write the pronoun separately, as in *Taga-Vigan ak*, because the hyphenated word is already long, and you'll see both styles.

## Saying you're a learner

> **Agad-adalak iti Ilokano.** — "I'm learning Ilocano."
> **Bassit laeng.** — "Just a little."
> **Saanko a maawatan.** / **Diak maawatan.** — "I don't understand."
> **Diak ammo.** — "I don't know."

**Agad-adal** is the "in progress" form of **agadal** (to study). You'll see how that doubling works in lesson 7.

## Leaving

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Innakon.** | I'm going now. |
| **Inkamin.** | We're going now. |
| **Sige.** | OK / bye (very common) |
| **Kastan.** / **Kasta pay.** | Till then / so long. |

The **-n** at the end of *innakon* and *inkamin* means "now, already". It's one of the most useful little pieces of Ilocano (lesson 10).

## Your first conversation

> **A:** Naimbag a bigat! Kumustaka?
> **B:** Naimbag met, agyamanak. Sika?
> **A:** Naimbag met. Ania ti naganmo?
> **B:** Siak ni Ana. Taga-Laoag ak. Sika?
> **A:** Siak ni Ben. Taga-California ak. Agad-adalak iti Ilokano.
> **B:** Nalaingka met! — "You're good at it!"
> **A:** Bassit laeng. Agyamanak!

**Nalaing** means "skilled, clever". Expect to hear *Nalaingka!* after three words; Ilocano speakers are generous with learners.

## Check yourself

1. You meet three people at once in the afternoon. What do you say?
2. A Tagalog-speaking friend hears you answer **Saan** and thinks you're asking "where?". What did you actually say?
3. Introduce yourself in two sentences: your name and where you're from.

## Key takeaways

- **Kumustaka?** (one person) / **Kumustakayo?** (several, or polite) → **Naimbag met, agyamanak.**
- **Naimbag a bigat / malem / rabii**, and **Naimbag nga aldaw**.
- **Wen** = yes, **Saan/Haan** = no (not "where"!).
- **Agyamanak** = thank you; **Dios ti agngina** = heartfelt thanks.
- **Ania ti naganmo?** → **Siak ni ___** or **Ti naganko ket ___.**
- **Taga-___ ak** = I'm from ___. **Innakon** = I'm off.
