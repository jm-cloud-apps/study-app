# Verbs, Part 1: ag-, -um-, and Aspect

> **The one-sentence version:** Ilocano verbs mark **aspect** (has it happened? is it ongoing?) rather than English tense: **nag-** / **-imm-** for "did", a doubled first syllable for "is doing", and the plain form plus the ending **-to** for "will do".

## Why it matters

Verbs are where a language comes alive. The good news is that Ilocano verbs **don't change for who's doing the action**: no *I go / she goes*. The pronoun endings from lesson 3 handle that. What changes is aspect, and the patterns are regular enough to learn in one sitting.

🔑 **Aspect, not tense.** English asks *when*: past, present, future. Ilocano asks *has the action happened, and is it ongoing?* That's why one form can cover "I ate" and "I've eaten".

## The three everyday actor verbs

This lesson covers verbs where the **doer is the focus**, the person the sentence is about. Three prefixes make most of them:

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **ag-** + root | **agbasa** | to read |
| **-um-** inside the root | **gumatang** (*g-um-atang*) | to buy |
| **mang-** + root | **mangan** | to eat |

A few common verbs use **ma-** instead: **maturog** (to sleep), **mapan** (to go).

**-um-** is an *infix*: it goes after the first consonant. *gatang* → **g-um-atang**; *inom* → **uminom** (to drink); *ay* → **umay** (to come).

## The four aspect forms

Here's **ag-** with *taray* (run), straight from the standard paradigm:

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutral** (infinitive, command, future base) | **agtaray** | to run |
| **Ongoing** (doubled syllable) | **agtartaray** | runs, is running |
| **Completed** (*ag-* → *nag-*) | **nagtaray** | ran, has run |
| **Past ongoing** | **nagtartaray** | was running, used to run |

And the same four slots for the other patterns:

| | ag- | -um- | mang- | ma- |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral | **agtaray** | **gumatang** | **mangrugi** (to start) | **maturog** |
| Ongoing | **agtartaray** | **gumatgatang** | **mangrugrugi** | **matmaturog** |
| Completed | **nagtaray** | **gimmatang** | **nangrugi** | **naturog** |
| Past ongoing | **nagtartaray** | **gimmatgatang** | **nangrugrugi** | **natmaturog** |

The rules behind the table:

- **Completed** swaps **m** for **n**: *ag-* → **nag-**, *mang-* → **nang-**, *ma-* → **na-**. For **-um-** it becomes **-imm-**: *gumatang* → **gimmatang**, *umay* → **immay**, *uminom* → **imminom**.
- **Ongoing** doubles the first syllable of the root: *taray* → **tar-taray**, *gatang* → **gat-gatang**, *adal* → **ad-adal**.

> 💡 When the root starts with a vowel, the doubled syllable takes a hyphen for the glottal stop: **agad-adal** (is studying), from *agadal*.

## Adding the doer

The pronoun ending goes on the verb, because it's the first word:

> **Nagbasaak.** — "I read." / "I've read."
> **Agbasbasaak.** — "I'm reading."
> **Gimmatangda.** — "They bought (something)."
> **Immay ni Lola.** — "Grandma came."

## The future: -to

There's no separate future form. Take the **neutral** verb, add the pronoun, then **-to** (**-nto** after a vowel):

> **Mapanakto.** — "I'll go."
> **Gumatangkanto?** — "Will you buy (it)?" (*-ka* ends in a vowel, so *-nto*)
> **Agawidkayonto kadi intono Sabado?** — "Are you (all) going home on Saturday?"

🔑 **Pronoun first, then -to**: *mapan-ak-to*, *agawid-kayo-nto*.

## Commands

The neutral form plus **-ka** (or **-kayo**) is a command:

> **Manganka.** — "Eat."
> **Umayka ditoy.** — "Come here."
> **Agdigoskayo.** — "Go take a bath, all of you."

With **-ta** or **-tayo** it becomes "let's": **Mangantayo!** (Let's eat!) **Aginanata bassit.** (Let's rest a bit, the two of us.)

## ag- or -um-? Same root, different feel

Many roots take both prefixes, with a difference in feel: **ag-** tends to mean repeated, lasting or deliberate, and **-um-** a single action or a change.

| ag- | -um- |
|---|---|
| **aginom** — to drink (habitually) | **uminom** — to drink (now, once) |
| **agtakder** — to be standing | **tumakder** — to stand up |
| **agbaknang** — to be rich | **bumaknang** — to become rich |

You don't need to predict this. Learn each verb with the prefix you hear it used with.

## Verbs to start with

| Verb | Meaning | Completed |
|---|---|---|
| **mangan** | eat | **nangan** |
| **uminom** | drink | **imminom** |
| **mapan** | go | **napan** |
| **umay** | come | **immay** |
| **agawid** | go home | **nagawid** |
| **agtrabaho** | work | **nagtrabaho** |
| **agadal** | study | **nagadal** |
| **agsao** | speak | **nagsao** |
| **agluto** | cook | **nagluto** |
| **maturog** | sleep | **naturog** |

> **Nanganka?** — "Have you eaten?" This is the classic Ilocano greeting as much as a question. Answer **Wen, nanganakon** ("Yes, I've eaten already"), and expect to be fed anyway.

## Key takeaways

- Actor-focus verbs: **ag-**, **-um-**, **mang-**, and a few with **ma-**.
- **Completed**: *ag- → nag-*, *mang- → nang-*, *ma- → na-*, *-um- → -imm-* (**immay**, **gimmatang**).
- **Ongoing**: double the root's first syllable (**agbasbasa**, **gumatgatang**).
- **Future**: neutral form + pronoun + **-to/-nto** (**Mapanakto**).
- Command: neutral + **-ka/-kayo**; "let's": + **-ta/-tayo** (**Mangantayo!**).
- **Nanganka?** → **Wen, nanganakon.**
