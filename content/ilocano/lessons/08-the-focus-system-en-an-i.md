# Verbs, Part 2: Focus with -en, -an and i-

> **The one-sentence version:** When the sentence is about the **thing acted on** rather than the doer, the verb changes: **-en** for the thing affected (*basaen*, read it), **i-** for the thing moved or given (*ited*, give it), and **-an** for the person or place on the receiving end (*suratan*, write to someone), with the doer in the **-ko** set.

## Why it matters

Ilocanos use these forms constantly, often more than the actor forms from lesson 7. "I know it", "What are you doing?", "I love you" and "Give it to me" are all built this way. If you know Tagalog's *-in / i- / -an*, you're nearly there: the logic is the same.

## Same event, two angles

> **Nagbasaak iti libro.** — "I read **a** book." (focus on me: -ak set; the book is indefinite, *iti*)
> **Binasak ti libro.** — "I read **the** book." (focus on the book: *ti*; I'm the doer in the -ko set, *-k*)

🔑 **Actor focus talks about the doer; the other foci talk about the thing.** And because the "thing" in focus is definite, switching to **-en** is also how you say *the* book rather than *a* book.

## -en: the thing affected

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral | **basaen** | to read (it) |
| Ongoing | **basbasaen** | is reading (it) |
| Completed (*-in-*) | **binasa** | read (it), has read |
| Past ongoing | **binasbasa** | was reading (it) |

**Completed** uses the infix **-in-** and drops the **-en**: *basa* → **b-in-asa**, *gatang* → **g-in-atang**, *aramid* (do) → **inaramid**.

> **Ginatangko ti kape.** — "I bought the coffee."
> **Inaramidna.** — "He/she did it."
> **Kitaem!** — "Look at it!"

## The endings fuse

When a **-ko** pronoun meets **-en** or **-an**, they merge, and it's worth learning the results as words:

| Verb ending | + -ko | + -mo | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-en** | **-ek** | **-em** | **aramidek** (I'll do it) · **Ania ti aramidem?** (What are you doing?) |
| **-an** | **-ak** | **-am** | **dinalusak** (I cleaned it) · **Nasuratam ida?** (Were you able to write to them?) |

> ⚠️ **dinalusak** ends in **-ak**, but here that's the fused *-an + -ko* ("by me"), not the *-ak* "I" from lesson 3. The verb tells you which: a **-an** verb plus **-ak** means "I did it to that place or person."

Other pronouns just attach: **basaenda** (they'll read it), **binasana** (he/she read it).

## i-: the thing moved, given or said

**i-** focuses on something that's **put, given, said, or sent somewhere**:

| Verb | Meaning | Completed |
|---|---|---|
| **ited** | give (it) | **inted** |
| **ibaga** | say, tell (it) | **imbaga** |
| **idulin** | put away, save (it) | **indulin** |
| **iruar** | put (it) outside | **inruar** |
| **ilukat** | open (it) | **inlukat** |

> **Itedmo kaniak.** — "Give it to me."
> **Imbaga ni Nanang a manganka.** — "Mom said that you should eat."
> **Indulin ti estudiante ti file.** — "The student saved the file."

**Completed** turns **i-** into **in-**; before *b* or *p* it usually becomes **im-** (**imbaga**), and you'll also hear **ing-** before *g* or *k*.

## -an: the person or place on the receiving end

**-an** focuses on the **goal**: who you write to, where you clean, what you clean something off.

| Verb | Meaning | Completed |
|---|---|---|
| **suratan** | write to (someone) | **sinuratan** |
| **dalusan** | clean (a place) | **dinalusan** |
| **labaan** | launder (clothes) | **linabaan** |
| **tulongan** | help (someone) | **tinulongan** |

> **Dinalusak ti sala.** — "I cleaned the living room."
> **Tulongannak.** — "Help me." (*tulongan* + *-nak*, "you, me")
> **Suratam ni Lola.** — "Write to Grandma."

> 💡 **i- + -an** together means "for someone": **igatangan** = to buy *for* someone. You'll recognise it in requests like **Maigatanganak ti libro?** ("Could you buy me a book?").

## Which focus to use?

| Question you're answering | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who's doing it? (thing is "a/some") | **ag- / -um- / mang-** | **Nangan iti saba.** — He ate a banana. |
| What happens to *this* thing? | **-en** | **Kinnanna ti saba.** — He ate the banana. |
| What's being given, put or said? | **i-** | **Inted ni Lola ti kuarta.** — Grandma gave the money. |
| Who or where is the target? | **-an** | **Sinuratanna ni Ben.** — She wrote to Ben. |

**Kinnan** is the completed form of **kanen** (to eat something), one of the few irregular-looking ones.

## Love, Ilocano style

> **Ay-ayatenka.** — "I love you."

Now you can read it: **ayaten** (to love someone, *-en* focus) in the ongoing form **ay-ayaten**, plus **-ka**, fused "I → you". The person loved is the focus, which is a nice thought to end a grammar lesson on.

## Key takeaways

- **-en** = the thing affected (*basaen* → **binasa**); **i-** = the thing given/put/said (*ited* → **inted**); **-an** = the person or place targeted (*suratan* → **sinuratan**).
- In these foci the doer takes the **-ko set**, and the focused thing is definite (**ti**).
- Completed: **-in-** (dropping *-en*), **in-/im-** for *i-*, **-in-…-an** for *-an*.
- Fusions: **-en + ko → -ek**, **-en + mo → -em**, **-an + ko → -ak**, **-an + mo → -am**.
- **Tulongannak** (help me), **Itedmo kaniak** (give it to me), **Ay-ayatenka** (I love you).
