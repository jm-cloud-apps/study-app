# Asking Questions

> **The one-sentence version:** Yes/no questions add **kadi** (or just rising intonation); information questions start with a question word (**ania**, **siasino**, **sadino**, **ayan**, **kaano**, **apay**, **kasano**, **mano**), and the pronoun ending jumps onto it.

## Why it matters

Questions are how a learner keeps a conversation alive: you ask, they talk, you listen. And Ilocano has a few everyday questions, like **Papanam?** and **Nanganka?**, that work as greetings as much as requests for information. Knowing them makes you sound like you belong.

## Yes/no questions

Take a statement and either raise your voice at the end, or add **kadi** after the first word:

> **Adda kape.** → **Adda kadi kape?** — "Is there coffee?"
> **Nabannogka.** → **Nabannogka kadi?** — "Are you tired?"

Answer **Wen** (yes) or **Saan / Haan** (no). Repeating the key word is also natural: **Adda.** ("There is.") **Awan.** ("There isn't.")

## The question words

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ania?** | What? | **Ania ti naganmo?** — What's your name? |
| **Siasino?** | Who? | **Siasino dayta?** — Who's that? |
| **Sadino?** | Where (to / at)? | **Sadino ti papanam?** — Where are you going? |
| **Ayan** | Where is (it)? | **Ayanna ti banio?** — Where's the bathroom? |
| **Kaano?** | When? | **Kaano ti fiesta?** — When's the fiesta? |
| **Apay?** | Why? | **Apay?** — Why? |
| **Kasano?** | How? | **Kasano ti mapan idiay?** — How do you get there? |
| **Mano?** | How many / how much? | **Mano daytoy?** — How much is this? |

> 💡 In speech **ania ti** often shortens to **aniat'**: **Aniat' naganmo?** You'll hear it far more than you'll read it.

## Question word, linker, verb

When a verb follows **apay** ("why") or **kasano** ("how"), join them with the linker **a / nga**, and the verb keeps its pronoun ending:

> **Apay nga immayka?** — "Why did you come?"
> **Kasano nga ammom?** — "How do you know?"

With **ania**, **siasino** and **sadino**, Ilocano usually uses **ti** instead: **Ania ti aramidem?** ("What are you doing?", literally "what is the thing you're doing?").

## Past or future "when"

**Kaano** pairs with the time words from the demonstratives:

> **Idi kaano?** — "When (in the past)?" — **Idi kalman.** — "Yesterday."
> **Inton kaano?** — "When (in the future)?" — **Intono bigat.** — "Tomorrow."

**idi** points back, **intono / inton** points forward (lesson 12).

## Everyday questions that are really greetings

| Question | Literally | A natural answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Kumustaka?** | How are you? | **Naimbag met.** |
| **Nanganka?** / **Nanganka kadin?** | Have you eaten (yet)? | **Wen, nanganakon.** |
| **Papanam?** | Where are you going? | **Idiay tiendaan.** — To the market. |
| **Ania ti aramidem?** | What are you doing? | **Awan, aginanaak laeng.** — Nothing, just resting. |
| **Taga-anoka?** | Where are you from? | **Taga-Laoag ak.** |

🔑 **Papanam?** and **Nanganka?** are friendly check-ins, not interrogations. A vague answer (**Idiay laeng**, "just over there") is perfectly polite.

| **Ania ti kayat a sawen ti "bigat"?** | What does "bigat" mean? |
| **In-inayad man.** | Slower, please. |
| **Ulitem man?** | Could you say it again? |

## Answering who/what/where

Short answers are fine, and the markers from lesson 4 come along:

> **Siasino ti immay?** — **Ni Ben.** — "Who came?" — "Ben."
> **Sadino ti papanam?** — **Idiay Vigan.** — "Where are you going?" — "Vigan."
> **Mano ti annakmo?** — **Tallo.** — "How many children do you have?" — "Three."

## Check yourself

1. Turn **Adda tubig** ("There's water") into a question in two ways.
2. Your neighbour calls out **Papanam?** as you walk past. What are they really doing, and how do you answer?
3. Ask "When did you arrive?" and "When will you go home?" using **idi** and **inton**.

## Key takeaways

- Yes/no: rising intonation or **kadi** after the first word.
- **Ania** what · **Siasino** who · **Sadino** where to/at · **Ayan** where is · **Kaano** when · **Apay** why · **Kasano** how · **Mano** how many/much.
- **Ania ti** → **aniat'** in speech.
- **Idi kaano?** (past) vs **Inton kaano?** (future).
- **Papanam?** and **Nanganka?** are greetings; answer lightly.
