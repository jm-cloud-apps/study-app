# Numbers, Time, and Money

> **The one-sentence version:** Ilocano has its own numbers (**maysa, dua, tallo…**), which people use for counting small things, but **clock time, dates and most prices use Spanish numbers** (*alas tres*, *dose ti Julio*, *beinte pesos*), and English numbers are common too.

## Why it matters

Numbers turn up in every errand: prices, ages, times, how many. Knowing which number system people actually use for what will save you more confusion than memorising every number.

## The native numbers

| # | Ilocano | # | Ilocano |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **maysa** | 6 | **innem** |
| 2 | **dua** | 7 | **pito** |
| 3 | **tallo** | 8 | **walo** |
| 4 | **uppat** | 9 | **siam** |
| 5 | **lima** | 10 | **sangapulo** |

Hold the doubled consonants (lesson 1): **tal-lo**, **up-pat**, **in-nem**.

Bigger numbers:

| # | Ilocano | How it's built |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | **sangapulo ket maysa** | ten and one |
| 20 | **duapulo** | two tens |
| 30 | **tallopulo** | three tens |
| 50 | **limapulo** | five tens |
| 100 | **sangagasut** | one hundred |
| 1,000 | **sangaribu** | one thousand |

**sanga-** means "one" in front of a unit, and **ket** ("and") joins the pieces.

## Counting things

The number comes before the noun, joined by the linker **a / nga** (lesson 6):

> **dua a balay** — "two houses"
> **tallo nga ubbing** — "three children"
> **maysa a kape** — "one coffee"

> 💡 Plural marking is optional after a number: **tallo nga ubing** and **tallo nga ubbing** are both heard. The number already says "more than one".

## First, second, third

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **umuna** | first |
| **maikadua** | second |
| **maikatlo** | third |
| **maudi** | last |

> **Daytoy ti umuna a panagbisitak.** — "This is my first visit."

## Where Spanish numbers take over

Ilocano, like Tagalog, absorbed Spanish numbers for three things:

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Clock time** | **alas tres** | three o'clock |
| | **alas siete y medya** | half past seven |
| **Dates** | **dose ti Julio** | the 12th of July |
| **Most prices and ages** | **beinte pesos** · **singkuenta** | 20 pesos · 50 |

> **Ania nga oras ita?** — "What time is it now?" — **Alas dos.** — "Two o'clock."

🔑 **Don't answer "what time is it?" with native numbers.** *Alas tres* is what everyone says; *tallo nga oras* means "three hours" (a duration), not "three o'clock".

English numbers are also everywhere, especially for prices, phone numbers and with younger speakers. If you blank on the Spanish, the English number is completely acceptable.

## Days and parts of the day

The days of the week are Spanish: **Lunes, Martes, Mierkoles, Huebes, Biernes, Sabado, Domingo**.

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **aldaw** | day |
| **bigat** | morning |
| **malem** | afternoon |
| **rabii** | night |
| **lawas** | week |
| **bulan** | month (also "moon") |
| **tawen** | year |

## Past, now, and future

These pair beautifully with the demonstratives from lesson 5:

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ita** | now; today |
| **itay** | a little while ago (earlier today) |
| **idi kalman** | yesterday |
| **intono bigat** | tomorrow |
| **intono rabii** | tonight |
| **idi** + time | in the past: **idi Lunes** (last Monday) |
| **intono / inton** + time | in the future: **intono Sabado** (on Saturday) |

🔑 **idi** looks back, **intono** looks forward. Put them in front of almost any time word.

## Money and shopping

The currency is the **piso** (peso), usually said with Spanish or English numbers.

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mano daytoy?** | How much is this? |
| **Mano dayta?** | How much is that (by you)? |
| **Nangina!** / **Nangina met!** | That's expensive! |
| **Nalaka.** | It's cheap. |
| **Adda kadi nalaklaka?** | Is there a cheaper one? |
| **Gumatangak iti dua.** | I'll buy two. |
| **Alaekon.** | I'll take it. |
| **Diak kayat.** | I don't want it. |
| **Agyamanak!** | Thanks! |

At a market stall:

> **A:** Manang, mano daytoy?
> **B:** Singkuenta.
> **A:** Ay, nangina met! Adda kadi nalaklaka?
> **B:** Daytoy, kuarenta.
> **A:** Sige, gumatangak iti dua. Agyamanak!

> 💡 Calling a vendor **Manang** (older woman) or **Manong** (older man) is normal and friendly, even if you've never met. Lesson 13 explains these words.

## Ages

> **Mano ti tawenmo?** — "How old are you?" (*how many are your years?*)
> **Beinte singko.** — "Twenty-five."

## Check yourself

1. Count from one to ten aloud, holding the doubled consonants.
2. Why is "three o'clock" **alas tres** and not *tallo*?
3. Say "I'll go home on Saturday" using **intono**.

## Key takeaways

- Native 1–10: **maysa, dua, tallo, uppat, lima, innem, pito, walo, siam, sangapulo**; **duapulo** (20), **sangagasut** (100), **sangaribu** (1,000).
- Number + linker + noun: **dua a balay**.
- **Spanish numbers** for clock time (**alas tres**), dates (**dose ti Julio**) and usually prices (**beinte pesos**). English numbers are fine too.
- **ita** now/today · **idi kalman** yesterday · **intono bigat** tomorrow; **idi** = past, **intono** = future.
- **Mano daytoy?** · **Nangina!** · **Adda kadi nalaklaka?**
