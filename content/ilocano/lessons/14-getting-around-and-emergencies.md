# Getting Around, Shopping, and Emergencies

> **The one-sentence version:** A short list of phrases gets you around the Ilocos: **Kasano ti mapan idiay ___?** (how do I get to ___?), **Dumiretso / Kumannigid / Kumannawan** (straight / left / right), **Para!** (stop here), **Manu ti plete?** (how much is the fare?), and for trouble, **Tulong!** and **Masapulko ti doktor.**

## Why it matters

The earlier lessons built the grammar. This one is the phrasebook you'll want on your phone on a trip to Vigan, Laoag or Baguio: asking the way, riding a jeepney or tricycle, buying things, and the handful of sentences you hope never to need. You'll recognise the grammar in every one of them now.

## Asking the way

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kasano ti mapan idiay ___?** | How do I get to ___? |
| **Ayanna ti ___?** | Where is ___? |
| **Adda kadi asideg a ___?** | Is there a ___ nearby? |
| **Mabalin nga pakitam kaniak ayanna idiay mapa?** | Can you show me where it is on the map? |

The answers you'll hear:

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dumiretsoka.** | Go straight ahead. |
| **Kumannigidka.** | Turn left. |
| **Kumannawanka.** | Turn right. |
| **Asideg laeng.** | It's just near. |
| **Adayo.** | It's far. |

> 💡 Notice the grammar: **dumiretso**, **kumannigid** and **kumannawan** are **-um-** verbs (lesson 7) with **-ka** on the end: a command, "you turn left". The roots are *diretso* (straight), *kannigid* (left) and *kannawan* (right).

## On a jeepney, tricycle or bus

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Manu ti plete inggana idiay ___?** | How much is the fare to ___? |
| **Maysa man a tiket a mapan ___.** | One ticket to ___, please. |
| **Sadino ti papanan daytoy a bus?** | Where does this bus go? |
| **Para!** / **Para, manong!** | Stop! (to get off) |
| **Ditoy laeng.** | Just here. |

🔑 **Para!** is the word everyone uses to stop a jeepney, all over the Philippines. Adding **manong** to the driver makes it friendly, and **Ditoy laeng** ("just here") tells him exactly where.

**manu** and **mano** are the same word ("how much"): you'll see both spellings.

## Shopping

You met the basics in lesson 12. A few more that come up constantly:

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Adda kadi dakdakkel?** / **basbassit?** | Do you have a bigger / smaller one? |
| **Adda kadi nalaklaka?** | Do you have a cheaper one? |
| **Alaekon.** | I'll take it. |
| **Diak kayat.** | I don't want it. |
| **Maysa pay.** | One more. |
| **Nalpasakon.** | I'm done / finished. |

**Alaekon** packs a lot in: **alaen** (take it, *-en* focus) + **-k** (by me, fused as **-ek**, lesson 8) + **-on** (now). "I'll take it now."

## At a restaurant or carinderia

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ania ti adda?** | What do you have? |
| **Kayatko ti ___.** | I'd like ___. |
| **Naimas!** | Delicious! |
| **Mano amin?** | How much is it altogether? |

> 💡 A **carinderia** is a small eatery with dishes in pots on display. Pointing at a pot and saying **Daytoy** ("this one") is completely normal.

## When something goes wrong

These are the phrases worth memorising before you need them:

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tulong!** | Help! |
| **Agannadka!** | Look out! / Be careful! |
| **Kasapulak ti tulongmo.** | I need your help. |
| **Naiyaw-awanak.** | I'm lost. |
| **Agsakitak.** | I'm sick. |
| **Masapulko ti doktor.** | I need a doctor. |
| **Naaksidenteak.** | I've had an accident. |
| **Agayabak iti pulis!** | I'll call the police! |
| **Agsardengka! Agtatakaw!** | Stop! Thief! |
| **Panawandak man!** | Leave me alone! |

You can read almost all of these with what you've learned: **Masapulko** (need, *-ko* set), **Agsakitak** (*ag-* + *sakit* + *-ak*), **Naiyaw-awanak** (a *na-* form + *-ak*, with the hyphenated glottal stop).

## At the doctor or pharmacy

| Ilocano | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Agsakit ti ulok.** | My head hurts. (*ulo* = head) |
| **Agsakit ti tianko.** | My stomach hurts. (*tian* = belly, stomach) |
| **Adda kadi agas para iti ___?** | Is there medicine for ___? (*agas* = medicine) |

**Agsakit ti ___ + my** ("the ___ of mine hurts") works for any body part, which makes it the most useful single pattern in this table. "My" is the **-ko** ending from lesson 3, so it follows the same rule: **-k** after a vowel (*ulo* → **ulok**), **-ko** after a consonant (*tian* → **tianko**).

| Body part | Ilocano | "My ___ hurts" |
|---|---|---|
| head | **ulo** | **Agsakit ti ulok.** |
| eye | **mata** | **Agsakit ti matak.** |
| ear | **lapayag** | **Agsakit ti lapayagko.** |
| nose | **agong** | **Agsakit ti agongko.** |
| tooth | **ngipen** | **Agsakit ti ngipenko.** |
| throat | **karabukob** | **Agsakit ti karabukobko.** |
| hand | **ima** | **Agsakit ti imak.** |
| belly, stomach | **tian** | **Agsakit ti tianko.** |

> 💡 You may also hear **buksit** for the stomach: the phrasebook's "stomach medicine" is **agas ti sakit iti buksit**.

## Taking your leave politely

> **Agyamanak unay, apo. Agaluadkayo.** — "Thank you very much. Take care (all of you / respectfully)."
> **Agpakadaakon.** — "I'm taking my leave now."

## Check yourself

1. Tell a jeepney driver to stop just here, politely.
2. Someone says **Kumannigidka, sa dumiretsoka.** Which way do you go?
3. Explain each part of **Alaekon**.

## Key takeaways

- **Kasano ti mapan idiay ___?** = How do I get to ___? → **Dumiretso / Kumannigid / Kumannawan** (straight / left / right).
- **Para!** stops a jeepney; **Ditoy laeng** = just here; **Manu ti plete?** = how much is the fare?
- Shopping: **Adda kadi dakdakkel / nalaklaka?** · **Alaekon** (I'll take it) · **Diak kayat**.
- Emergencies: **Tulong!** · **Agannadka!** · **Naiyaw-awanak** (I'm lost) · **Masapulko ti doktor**.
- **Agsakit ti ___-k / -ko** = my ___ hurts: **ulok** (head), **matak** (eye), **ngipenko** (tooth), **tianko** (stomach).
- Leave with **Agyamanak unay, apo. Agaluadkayo.**
