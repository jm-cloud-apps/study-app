# Wanting, Needing, Being Able, and Saying No

> **The one-sentence version:** **kayat** (want), **masapul** (need), **mabalin** (may, can) and **ammo** (know) take the **-ko** endings; **maka-** turns a verb into "be able to"; and Ilocano has three ways to say "not": **saan** (with a linker), **di** (without), and **awan** (for "there isn't").

## Why it matters

"I want…", "I need…", "Can I…?", "I don't know" and "I can't" are survival phrases, and the negatives are where learners slip most. Get this lesson and you can ask for things, decline them politely, and admit you're lost, which is half of real conversation.

## Want, need, may, know

These words behave like the **-en** verbs from lesson 8: the person who wants or knows is in the **-ko** set, and the thing wanted is marked with **ti**.

| Word | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **kayat** | **Kayatko ti kape.** | I want coffee. |
| **masapul** | **Masapulko ti tulong.** | I need help. |
| **ammo** | **Ammok.** / **Ammom?** | I know. / Do you know? |
| **mabalin** | **Mabalin kadi?** | May I? Is it possible? |

To want or need to *do* something, follow with **ti** or the linker and the neutral verb:

> **Kayatko ti mangan.** — "I want to eat."
> **Masapul nga agadalka.** — "You need to study."
> **Kayatmo ti umay?** — "Do you want to come?"

> 💡 **ammo** + **-ko** is **ammok**, because *ammo* ends in a vowel (lesson 3). Same with **Ammom?** ("Do you know?").

## Being able: maka-

Add **maka-** to the root and you get "able to":

| Verb | Able to… | Completed ("was able to") |
|---|---|---|
| **maturog** (sleep) | **makaturog** | **nakaturog** |
| **gumatang** (buy) | **makagatang** | **nakagatang** |
| **agsao** (speak) | **makasao** | **nakasao** |

> **Agsasaoka iti Ilokano?** — "Do you speak Ilocano?" (the ongoing form of *agsao*) — **Bassit laeng.** — "Just a little."
> **Diak makapagsao iti Ilokano.** — "I can't speak Ilocano." (*makapag-* is the "able to" form of *ag-* verbs)
> **Saanak a makaturog.** — "I can't sleep."
> **Nakagatangak iti tiket.** — "I managed to buy a ticket."

For **-en / -an / i-** verbs the "able to" form is **ma-**: *kitaen* (see it) → **makita** (be able to see it), *ibaga* → **maibaga**, and *awatan* → **maawatan** (understand):

> **Maawatam?** — "Do you understand?"
> **Saanko a maawatan.** — "I don't understand."
> **Nakitak.** — "I saw it." (literally "I was able to see it": seeing usually uses this form)

## Saying "not": three words, three jobs

| Word | Negates | Linker? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **saan / haan** | verbs, adjectives, nouns | **yes** (*a/nga*) | **Saanak a nabannog.** — I'm not tired. |
| **di** | verbs (and a few fixed words) | **no** | **Diak napan.** — I didn't go. |
| **awan** | existence, having | (+ *ti*) | **Awan ti kuarta.** — There's no money. |

🔑 **The pronoun jumps onto the negative.** Because **saan** or **di** is now the first word, the ending moves to it:

> **Napanak.** → **Saanak a napan.** / **Diak napan.** — "I didn't go."
> **Estudianteka.** → **Saanka nga estudiante.** — "You're not a student."
> **Nalaingak.** → **Saanak a nalaing.** — "I'm not clever."

Remember **di** + **-ko** = **diak** (not *diko*): **Diak ammo** ("I don't know"), **Diak kayat** ("I don't want to").

## Don't!

For a negative command, put the pronoun on **di**: **dika** (you), **dikayo** (you all). With a "me" in it, the fused ending from lesson 3 appears:

> **Dika mapan.** — "Don't go."
> **Dinak ig-igaman.** — "Don't touch me."

A gentler way, closer to "please don't", uses **saan** with **koma** ("I wish, should"):

> **Saanmo koma iparuar ti aso.** — "Please don't take the dog out."

## Small but useful negatives

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Saan pay.** | Not yet. (for "not there yet", **Awan pay.**) |
| **Saan unay.** | Not very / not really. |
| **Saan a mabalin.** | Not allowed / can't be done. |
| **Awan ti problema.** | No problem. |
| **Awan ti immay.** | Nobody came. |
| **Awan ti saan nga immay.** | Everybody came. (*there's no one who didn't come*) |

## Declining politely

Ilocanos rarely say a bare **Saan** to an offer, especially of food. Soften it:

> **Saan, agyamanak.** — "No, thank you."
> **Nanganakon, agyamanak.** — "I've already eaten, thanks." (the classic polite refusal)
> **Bassit laeng, apo.** — "Just a little, please." (when refusing outright would be rude)

## Check yourself

1. Negate **Nangan ni Ben** with both **saan** and **di**. Which one needs a linker?
2. Why is "There's no rice" **Awan ti bagas** and not *Saan a bagas*?
3. Say "I want to go home" (*agawid*) and then "I can't go home."

## Key takeaways

- **kayat, masapul, ammo, mabalin** take **-ko** endings: **Kayatko ti kape. Ammok.**
- **maka-** = able to (**makaturog**, **makasao**); for *-en/-an/i-* verbs it's **ma-** (**makita**, **maawatan**).
- **saan/haan** + linker; **di** without; **awan** for existence.
- Pronouns move onto the negative: **Saanak a…**, **Diak…** (never *diko*).
- **Dika…** = don't (**Dinak ig-igaman** = don't touch me). **Saan pay** = not yet. **Diak ammo** = I don't know.
