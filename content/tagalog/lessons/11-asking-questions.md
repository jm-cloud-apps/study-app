# Asking Questions

> **The one-sentence version:** Tagalog question words go at the front, most of them link to the rest of the sentence with **ang** (usually contracted to **'ng**), and yes/no questions just need **ba**.

## Why it matters

Questions are how a beginner learns fastest — every question you can ask turns a stranger into a teacher. This lesson is small, high-yield, and immediately usable.

## The question words

| Tagalog | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ano** | what | **Ano ito?** — What's this? |
| **Sino** | who | **Sino siya?** — Who is he/she? |
| **Kanino** | whose, to whom | **Kanino ito?** — Whose is this? |
| **Saan** | where (action) | **Saan ka pupunta?** — Where are you going? |
| **Nasaan** | where (location) | **Nasaan ang banyo?** — Where's the bathroom? |
| **Kailan** | when | **Kailan ka aalis?** — When are you leaving? |
| **Bakit** | why | **Bakit?** — Why? |
| **Paano** | how (in what way) | **Paano?** — How? |
| **Ilan** | how many | **Ilan kayo?** — How many of you are there? |
| **Magkano** | how much (price) | **Magkano ito?** — How much is this? |
| **Alin** | which | **Alin ang gusto mo?** — Which one do you want? |
| **Gaano** | how (degree) | **Gaano katagal?** — How long? |

## The 'ng contraction

When a question word is followed by **ang**, the two fuse in speech. You'll see all three spellings, and they're all the same thing:

> **Ano ang pangalan mo?** → **Anong pangalan mo?** → **Ano'ng pangalan mo?**

> **Sino ang kasama mo?** → **Sino'ng kasama mo?** — "Who are you with?"
> **Ano'ng ginagawa mo?** — "What are you doing?"
> **Ano'ng oras na?** — "What time is it?"

Use whichever spelling you like; **ano'ng** and **anong** are both common online.

## Yes/no questions: just add ba

No word-order change needed. **ba** goes after the first word or phrase:

> **Masarap ba?** — "Is it good?"
> **Kumain ka na ba?** — "Have you eaten yet?"
> **Pwede ba akong umupo?** — "May I sit?"
> **May tubig ba kayo?** — "Do you have water?"

In fast, casual speech **ba** is often dropped and intonation carries the question — exactly like English *"You've eaten?"*

## Answering

| Question type | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| General | **Oo** | **Hindi** |
| Polite | **Opo** | **Hindi po** |
| About having / existence | **Meron** / **Oo, meron** | **Wala** |
| "Yet?" questions | **Oo, tapos na** | **Hindi pa** |

> ⚠️ Answer **"May tubig ba kayo?"** with **Wala**, not *Hindi* — questions about having something get **meron/wala**. This is the same three-negatives rule from lesson 9, showing up in conversation.

**Hindi pa** ("not yet") is worth its own note. It's softer than a flat "no" and often the socially preferred answer — *"Kumain ka na ba?" "Hindi pa."*

## Asking about people

> **Sino ka?** — "Who are you?" *(direct; can sound abrupt)*
> **Sino po sila?** — "Who are they?" / "Who is this?" *(polite — note the plural for respect)*
> **Sino'ng kasama mo?** — "Who are you with?"
> **Kanino ang bag na ito?** — "Whose bag is this?"

## Asking why, and answering with kasi

> **Bakit ka umalis?** — "Why did you leave?"
> **Kasi may trabaho ako.** — "Because I had work."

**Bakit** on its own — *"Bakit?"* — is a complete question, and **Kasi…** is how essentially every explanation starts. If you learn only one pairing from this lesson, make it **bakit → kasi**.

## Degrees: the gaano ka- pattern

**Gaano** combines with **ka-** + a root to ask "how [adjective]?":

| Question | English |
|---|---|
| **Gaano katagal?** | How long (in time)? |
| **Gaano kalayo?** | How far? |
| **Gaano kalaki?** | How big? |
| **Gaano kadalas?** | How often? |

Note it's the bare root with **ka-**, not the *ma-* adjective: *kalayo*, not *~~kamalayo~~*.

## The questions you'll actually use this week

| Tagalog | English |
|---|---|
| **Ano'ng ibig sabihin ng ___?** | What does ___ mean? |
| **Paano sabihin ang ___ sa Tagalog?** | How do you say ___ in Tagalog? |
| **Pwede mo bang ulitin?** | Can you repeat that? |
| **Pwede bang dahan-dahan?** | Could you go slowly? |
| **Ano'ng tawag dito?** | What's this called? |
| **Marunong ka bang mag-English?** | Do you speak English? |
| **Hindi ko maintindihan.** | I don't understand. |
| **Sandali lang po.** | One moment, please. |

> 💡 **Ano'ng ibig sabihin ng ___?** is the single most valuable sentence in this lesson. Learn it as one unbroken chunk and use it constantly. Filipinos will happily explain — and will usually throw in three more words you didn't ask for, which is exactly what you want.

## A conversation you could have tomorrow

> **A: Kumusta ka?** — How are you?
> **B: Mabuti naman, ikaw?** — I'm good, and you?
> **A: Okay lang. Saan ka pupunta?** — I'm fine. Where are you going?
> **B: Sa palengke. Gusto mo bang sumama?** — To the market. Do you want to come along?
> **A: Sige! Anong oras?** — Sure! What time?
> **B: Mamaya, alas-tres.** — Later, at three.

**Sige** means "okay / go ahead / sure," and it's everywhere. **Mabuti naman** is the standard answer to *Kumusta* — as is **Okay lang**, which is more casual and, honestly, more common.

## Check yourself

1. What's the difference between **Saan** and **Nasaan**? Give an example of each.
2. Someone asks **"May kotse ka ba?"** and you don't have one. What's the right one-word answer, and why isn't it *hindi*?
3. Build the question "What are you cooking?" using the **'ng** contraction.

## Key takeaways

- Question words go **first**: *Ano, Sino, Kanino, Saan, Nasaan, Kailan, Bakit, Paano, Ilan, Magkano, Alin, Gaano.*
- **Question word + ang** contracts to **'ng**: *Ano'ng pangalan mo?*
- Yes/no questions just take **ba** — no word-order change; in casual speech, intonation alone works.
- Answers: **Oo/Hindi**, politely **Opo/Hindi po**, about having **Meron/Wala**, and the very useful **Hindi pa**.
- **Bakit** is answered with **kasi**.
- **Gaano ka-** + root asks about degree: *Gaano katagal?*
- Memorize **Ano'ng ibig sabihin ng ___?** as one chunk — it turns every conversation into a lesson.
