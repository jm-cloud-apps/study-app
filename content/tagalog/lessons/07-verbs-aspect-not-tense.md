# Verbs, Part 1: Aspect, Not Tense

> **The one-sentence version:** Tagalog verbs don't mark past/present/future — they mark whether the action is **finished**, **ongoing**, or **not started yet** — and you build all three by adding an affix and repeating a syllable.

## Why it matters

This is where Tagalog stops being a vocabulary exercise and becomes a language. It's also where most learners stall, because they keep hunting for a past tense that isn't there. Ten minutes spent getting the *idea* right saves months of confusion.

## The mental shift

English asks: *when did it happen?* Tagalog asks: **how far along is it?**

| Aspect | Meaning | Rough English |
|---|---|---|
| **Completed** | It's done | ate, has eaten, did eat |
| **Progressive** | It's underway (or habitual) | is eating, eats, was eating |
| **Contemplated** | It hasn't started | will eat, is going to eat |

🔑 **Completed ≠ past tense.** *Kumain na ako* means "I've already eaten" — the eating is finished, whether that was this morning or ten years ago. And the progressive form covers both "is eating" *and* "eats every day." Time itself gets specified separately, with words like **kahapon** (yesterday), **ngayon** (now), and **bukas** (tomorrow).

## The machinery: an affix plus a repeated syllable

Every Tagalog verb is a **root** wearing an **affix**. Two affixes account for a huge share of everyday verbs: **-um-** and **mag-**.

Both make the aspects the same way — with **reduplication**, which just means repeating the root's first consonant-plus-vowel. *kain* → *ka-kain*. *luto* → *lu-luto*. *trabaho* → *ta-trabaho*.

### -um- verbs

**-um-** is an *infix* — it goes **inside** the word, right after the first consonant.

Take **kain** (eat): k + **um** + ain = **kumain**.

| Aspect | Form | How it's built |
|---|---|---|
| Basic ("to eat") | **kumain** | root + *-um-* |
| **Completed** | **kumain** | identical to the basic form |
| **Progressive** | **kumakain** | *-um-* + reduplication |
| **Contemplated** | **kakain** | reduplication only, no *-um-* |

More of the same, so you can see the shape repeat:

| Root | Basic / Completed | Progressive | Contemplated |
|---|---|---|---|
| **punta** (go) | **pumunta** | **pumupunta** | **pupunta** |
| **inom** (drink) | **uminom** | **umiinom** | **iinom** |
| **alis** (leave) | **umalis** | **umaalis** | **aalis** |
| **upo** (sit) | **umupo** | **umuupo** | **uupo** |

> 💡 When the root starts with a **vowel**, *-um-* has nothing to go behind, so it just sits in front: *inom* → **uminom**.

> ⚠️ Yes — the basic form and the completed form of *-um-* verbs are **identical**. **Kumain ako** can be "I ate" or, as a command with *ka*, "Eat." Context and the particles around it sort it out, and this ambiguity genuinely doesn't bother anyone.

### mag- verbs

**mag-** is a prefix, and its completed form swaps the *m* for an *n*:

| Root | Basic | **Completed** | **Progressive** | **Contemplated** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **luto** (cook) | **magluto** | **nagluto** | **nagluluto** | **magluluto** |
| **trabaho** (work) | **magtrabaho** | **nagtrabaho** | **nagtatrabaho** | **magtatrabaho** |
| **laro** (play) | **maglaro** | **naglaro** | **naglalaro** | **maglalaro** |
| **aral** (study) | **mag-aral** | **nag-aral** | **nag-aaral** | **mag-aaral** |

The rule in one line: **m- = not yet done, n- = done.** Then reduplicate for the progressive.

> 💡 Write a hyphen after **mag-** when the root starts with a vowel: **mag-aral**, **nag-aaral**. It keeps *mag-aral* from being misread as *ma-garal*.

## Which affix does a verb take?

Honest answer: **you memorize it with the word.** There are tendencies — **mag-** often suggests a more deliberate or sustained activity, **-um-** a simpler one-off action — but the tendencies leak, and native speakers aren't applying a rule. Learn **kumain**, **nagluto**, **pumunta**, **nagtrabaho** as whole words, the way you learned English irregular verbs.

Some roots take both, with different meanings, which is a real (if occasional) trap: *bumili* is "to buy," while *magbili* leans toward "to sell." Don't hunt for these; meet them as they arrive.

## Where the verb goes, and where everyone else goes

Verb first. Then the short pronouns, then everything else.

> **Kumain ako.** — "I ate."
> **Kumain ako ng mangga.** — "I ate a mango."
> **Kumakain ako ng mangga sa bahay.** — "I'm eating a mango at home."
> **Kakain kami sa labas bukas.** — "We'll eat out tomorrow."

Notice the object takes **ng** — the mango isn't the spotlight, *I* am (**ako**, from the ANG set). That's the marker system from lesson 4 doing its job. In the next lesson we'll flip the spotlight onto the mango, and the verb will change shape to match.

## Pinning down actual time

Since the verb doesn't carry time, you add it:

| Tagalog | English |
|---|---|
| **kahapon** | yesterday |
| **ngayon** | now, today |
| **bukas** | tomorrow |
| **kanina** | earlier today |
| **mamaya** | later today |
| **noong isang araw** | the other day |
| **tuwing Lunes** | every Monday |

> **Nagtrabaho ako kahapon.** — "I worked yesterday."
> **Magtatrabaho ako bukas.** — "I'll work tomorrow."
> **Nagtatrabaho ako tuwing Sabado.** — "I work every Saturday."

That last one shows the progressive doing habitual duty — no separate form needed.

## Two particles that do a lot of tense-like work

- **na** — "already," "now"
- **pa** — "still," "yet"

> **Kumain ka na?** — "Have you eaten (already)?" *— the most Filipino question there is*
> **Kumain na ako.** — "I've already eaten."
> **Hindi pa ako kumakain.** — "I haven't eaten yet."
> **Kumakain pa siya.** — "He/She is still eating."

**Kumain ka na?** deserves a note: it's a greeting as much as a question. Asking whether someone has eaten is how care gets expressed. Answering **"Hindi pa"** in a Filipino household will produce food within minutes.

## Key takeaways

- Tagalog verbs mark **aspect** (completed / progressive / contemplated), not tense.
- **Reduplication** — repeating the root's first consonant+vowel — builds the progressive and contemplated forms.
- **-um-** is an infix after the first consonant: *kumain, kumakain, kakain*. Basic and completed forms are identical.
- **mag-** is a prefix: **m- = not done, n- = done** — *magluto, nagluto, nagluluto, magluluto*.
- Which affix a root takes is **lexical** — memorize it with the verb.
- Actual time comes from separate words: **kahapon, ngayon, bukas, mamaya**.
- **na** = already; **pa** = still/yet. **Kumain ka na?** is a greeting.
