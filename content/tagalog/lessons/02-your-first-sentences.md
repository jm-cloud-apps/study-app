# Your First Sentences: Saying What Things Are

> **The one-sentence version:** Tagalog makes statements by putting two pieces side by side — *description* + *the thing* — with no verb "to be" in between, so you can build real sentences on day one.

## Why it matters

The simplest Tagalog sentence type is also one of the most useful. Introducing yourself, describing food, saying where you're from, asking who someone is — all of it runs on this one pattern. Learn it properly now and you'll reuse it every single day.

## The pattern: two slots, no glue

An English sentence needs a linking verb: *"I **am** Filipino." "The food **is** delicious."* Tagalog just puts the two parts next to each other:

> **Filipino ako.**
> *Filipino / I* → "I'm Filipino."

> **Masarap ang pagkain.**
> *delicious / the food* → "The food is delicious."

> **Guro siya.**
> *teacher / he-she* → "He's a teacher." / "She's a teacher."

🔑 **Slot 1 is what you're saying. Slot 2 is who or what you're saying it about.** Tagalog leads with the news.

Notice something in that third example: **siya** covers both "he" and "she." Tagalog pronouns don't mark gender at all — one of several places where the language is simpler than English. Context tells you which, and if it doesn't, nobody minds.

## Meeting people: si and ang

Two little words introduce the "who/what" slot, and choosing between them is easy:

- **si** goes before a **person's name**.
- **ang** goes before **everything else**.

| Tagalog | English |
|---|---|
| **Ako si Miguel.** | I'm Miguel. |
| **Si Ate Joy ang guro.** | Ate Joy is the teacher. |
| **Masarap ang adobo.** | The adobo is delicious. |
| **Maganda ang Palawan.** | Palawan is beautiful. |

> ⚠️ Place names take **ang**, not **si** — **si** is for people. *Maganda ang Palawan*, never *~~Maganda si Palawan~~*.

For more than one person, **si** becomes **sina**: **Sina Miguel at Joy** — "Miguel and Joy." And **ang** becomes **ang mga** for plurals (**mga** is pronounced *ma-NGA*):

> **Masarap ang mga mangga.** — "The mangoes are delicious."

**mga** is the general plural marker, and it's optional in a way English plurals aren't — if the number is obvious, Filipinos often leave it out.

## Introducing yourself, for real

Here's a small exchange you could genuinely use this week:

> **A: Kumusta! Ako si Anna. Ano'ng pangalan mo?**
> *Hi! I'm Anna. What's your name?*
>
> **B: Ako naman si Miguel. Taga-saan ka?**
> *I'm Miguel. Where are you from?*
>
> **A: Taga-Canada ako. Ikaw?**
> *I'm from Canada. You?*
>
> **B: Taga-Cavite ako.**
> *I'm from Cavite.*

Three things worth pocketing:

- **Kumusta?** — "How are you?" / "Hi." It comes from Spanish *¿Cómo está?*, which is a good early hint at how much Spanish is buried in Tagalog. You'll also see it written **kamusta**; both are fine.
- **Taga-** + place = "from that place." **Taga-Maynila ako.** Wonderfully simple.
- **Ano'ng pangalan mo?** — "What's your name?" **Ano'ng** is the everyday contraction of **anong** / **ano ang**. People write all three.

> 💡 **naman** in *"Ako naman si Miguel"* softens the sentence and marks a turn — roughly "and I'm Miguel," or "me, I'm Miguel." Tagalog is full of these tiny particles, and they're most of what makes speech sound natural rather than robotic. There's a whole lesson on them later (lesson 10); for now just notice them.

## Saying no: hindi

Put **hindi** in front of the first slot:

| Tagalog | English |
|---|---|
| **Hindi ako Filipino.** | I'm not Filipino. |
| **Hindi masarap ang pagkain.** | The food isn't delicious. |
| **Hindi siya guro.** | He/She isn't a teacher. |

In fast speech **hindi** often shortens to **'di**: **Di ako sigurado.** — "I'm not sure." You'll hear this constantly.

## Asking a yes/no question: ba

There's no need to change the word order. Drop **ba** in after the first word or phrase:

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| **Masarap ang pagkain.** | **Masarap ba ang pagkain?** — Is the food delicious? |
| **Guro siya.** | **Guro ba siya?** — Is he/she a teacher? |
| **Filipino ka.** | **Filipino ka ba?** — Are you Filipino? |

Answer with **Oo** (yes) or **Hindi** (no) — and in polite company, **Opo** and **Hindi po**, which we'll come to in lesson 13.

> 💡 In casual speech people often skip **ba** entirely and just use rising intonation, exactly like English *"You're Filipino?"* Both are correct. **ba** is a little clearer.

## A starter vocabulary

Enough to make dozens of sentences with the pattern you just learned:

| Describing | | People & things | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **maganda** | beautiful | **guro** | teacher |
| **masarap** | delicious | **estudyante** | student |
| **mabait** | kind | **doktor** | doctor |
| **malaki** | big | **bahay** | house |
| **maliit** | small | **pagkain** | food |
| **mainit** | hot | **tubig** | water |
| **malamig** | cold | **kaibigan** | friend |
| **mahal** | expensive / beloved | **trabaho** | work, job |

> 💡 Spot the pattern: most of the describing words start with **ma-**. That's not a coincidence — **ma-** is a prefix that turns a quality-noun into an adjective (*ganda* "beauty" → *maganda* "beautiful"). Lesson 6 makes this systematic.

And yes — **mahal** really means both "expensive" and "dear/beloved." *Mahal kita* is "I love you"; *Ang mahal!* is "That's so expensive!" Context does the work, and Filipinos enjoy the pun as much as you'd expect.

## Check yourself

1. How would you say "The house is big"? And "The house isn't big"?
2. Why is it **si Miguel** but **ang Palawan**?
3. Turn **Mabait siya** into a yes/no question, two different ways.

## Key takeaways

- The basic sentence is **description + who/what**, with **no verb "to be."**
- **si** marks a person's name (plural **sina**); **ang** marks everything else (plural **ang mga**).
- **siya** means both "he" and "she" — Tagalog pronouns don't mark gender.
- **Taga-** + place = "from (place)"; **Kumusta?** = "How are you?"
- Negate with **hindi** in front (often shortened to **'di**).
- Make a yes/no question by adding **ba** after the first word — or just use rising intonation.
- Most adjectives start with **ma-**.
