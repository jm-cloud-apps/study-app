# Describing Things: ma- Adjectives and the Linker

> **The one-sentence version:** Most Tagalog adjectives are built with **ma-**, and when you attach one to a noun you must glue them together with a linker — **na** or **-ng** — which is the single most common thing beginners forget.

## Why it matters

Adjectives are how a beginner sounds like a person rather than a phrasebook. And the linker is a genuine make-or-break detail: leave it out and you sound like a robot; use it and you suddenly sound like you've studied.

## The ma- pattern

Take a noun of quality, add **ma-**, and you get the adjective:

| Root (a quality) | + ma- | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **ganda** (beauty) | **maganda** | beautiful |
| **bait** (kindness) | **mabait** | kind |
| **sarap** (deliciousness) | **masarap** | delicious |
| **init** (heat) | **mainit** | hot |
| **lamig** (coldness) | **malamig** | cold |
| **laki** (size) | **malaki** | big |
| **liit** (smallness) | **maliit** | small |
| **bilis** (speed) | **mabilis** | fast |
| **bagal** (slowness) | **mabagal** | slow |
| **dumi** (dirt) | **madumi** | dirty |
| **linis** (cleanliness) | **malinis** | clean |
| **hirap** (difficulty) | **mahirap** | difficult; poor |

Roughly, **ma-** means "having a lot of this quality." Once you see it, you'll spot it everywhere.

Not everything follows the pattern, though. A solid set of everyday adjectives simply don't take **ma-**, and you learn them as they come:

| Tagalog | English | | Tagalog | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **bago** | new | | **luma** | old (things) |
| **matanda** | old (people) | | **bata** | young |
| **pangit** | ugly, bad | | **mabuti** | good, well |
| **masaya** | happy, fun | | **malungkot** | sad |
| **tahimik** | quiet | | **maingay** | noisy |
| **mura** | cheap | | **mahal** | expensive |

## The linker: na and -ng

To attach an adjective to a noun, you need a linker between them. Which one depends only on the sound at the end of the first word:

| First word ends in… | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a **vowel** | **-ng** (attached) | **maganda** + **-ng** → **magandang bahay** |
| the letter **n** | **-g** (attached) | **iyan** + **-g** → **iyang libro** |
| any other consonant | **na** (separate word) | **mabait** + **na** → **mabait na tao** |

> **magandang babae** — a beautiful woman
> **mabait na tao** — a kind person
> **malaking bahay** — a big house
> **masarap na pagkain** — delicious food

🔑 **The order is reversible, and the linker moves with it.** *magandang babae* and *babaeng maganda* both mean "beautiful woman." The linker always sits *between* the two words, attaching to whichever one comes first.

> ⚠️ This is the mistake to watch. *~~Maganda bahay~~* is missing its glue. It's understandable, but it marks you instantly as a learner — and it's a genuinely easy fix.

The linker isn't only for adjectives. It joins all sorts of things to nouns, and you've already met it:

> **Mayroon akong bahay.** — "I have a house." *(ako + -ng)*
> **isang tao** — one person *(isa + -ng)*
> **Gusto kong kumain.** — "I want to eat." *(ko + -ng)*

## Saying "very": four ways, in order of usefulness

**1. sobrang** — the everyday intensifier, extremely common:

> **Sobrang sarap!** — "So delicious!"
> **Sobrang init ngayon.** — "It's so hot today."

**2. ang + root** — an exclamation, and it's the root, not the *ma-* form:

> **Ang ganda!** — "So beautiful!"
> **Ang sarap!** — "So good!"

That **ang** is not the marker from lesson 4; it's a separate exclamatory word that happens to look the same. Filipinos say this constantly, especially about food.

**3. napaka- + root** — stronger, slightly more formal or written:

> **Napakaganda ng bahay.** — "The house is very beautiful."

**4. masyado** — "too," in the negative sense of excessive:

> **Masyadong mahal.** — "Too expensive."

## Comparing things

**More:** put **mas** in front.

> **Mas maganda ang Palawan.** — "Palawan is more beautiful."
> **Mas mabait siya kaysa sa akin.** — "He/She is kinder than me."

"Than" is **kaysa sa** (or **kaysa kay** before a name):

> **Mas mahal ito kaysa kay Miguel.** — "This is more expensive than Miguel's."

**Most:** **pinaka-** attached to the front.

> **Pinakamasarap** — the most delicious.
> **Siya ang pinakamabait sa klase.** — "She's the kindest in the class."

**The same as:** **kasing-** attached to the root.

> **Kasingganda mo siya.** — "She's as beautiful as you."

> 💡 In real speech, English creeps in constantly here: *"Mas nice 'to,"* *"sobrang cute,"* *"pinaka-favorite ko."* That's not lazy Tagalog — it's how Metro Manila actually talks. Lesson 14 takes this seriously.

## A note on plural adjectives

Tagalog can pluralize an adjective by repeating its first syllable: **maganda** → **magaganda**, **malaki** → **malalaki**.

> **Magaganda ang mga bahay.** — "The houses are beautiful."

It's optional, and leaving it out is perfectly normal in speech. File it under "recognize now, use later."

## Key takeaways

- Most adjectives are **ma-** + a quality root: *ganda* → **maganda**.
- Attaching an adjective to a noun requires a **linker**: **-ng** after a vowel, **-g** after *n*, **na** after other consonants.
- Word order is reversible — **magandang babae** = **babaeng maganda**.
- The linker also joins pronouns and numbers to what follows: *akong*, *isang*, *kong*.
- Intensifiers: **sobrang** (everyday), **ang** + root (exclamation), **napaka-** (stronger), **masyado** (too much).
- **mas** … **kaysa sa/kay** = more … than; **pinaka-** = most; **kasing-** = as … as.
